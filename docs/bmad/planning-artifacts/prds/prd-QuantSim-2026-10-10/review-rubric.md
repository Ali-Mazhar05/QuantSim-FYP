# PRD Quality Review — QuantSim Iteration 1: Delivery Pipeline and Market Data Ingestion

## Overall verdict
This is a strong PRD. It has a real thesis (trustworthy data plus a safe way to ship it), a precise glossary, testable consequences on almost every FR, honest counter-metrics and Open Questions that are genuinely open. The main risk is not in any single requirement. It is that 31 FRs are all marked in scope for a three-person team with no priority order or cut line, while the addendum crosswalk shows most of the market engine "Not started" or "Draft code only". Two success metrics (SM-3, SM-4) also depend on a hosting setup and a Mid-I date that do not exist yet. Fix the scope ordering and re-base SM-4 and the PRD is ready to feed story creation.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions. Examples: "PostgreSQL queue (D6)", "Flag, configurable to reject (D4)", and the source-reconciliation table in addendum §E, which picks a position on each conflict instead of smoothing it over. The Open Questions (§10) are real and each names what it blocks. OQ1 says the missing Mid-I date "blocks the SM-3 and SM-4 deadlines". OQ2 ties the precision choice to migration I1-20. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at actual tensions: the MID "GitHub Testing" error, and correlation versus TDA.

What a decision-maker cannot do is decide what to cut. §8.1 puts FR-1 to FR-31 in scope as one block. Only correlation and tracing are named as cuttable ("first to cut"). Addendum §A shows FR-7, FR-21 and FR-22 "Not started", FR-13 "market not started", and FR-20 and FR-23 to FR-28 "Draft code only". The PRD never says which FRs make up the critical path for SM-1, or what gets dropped if the market service slips. A supervisor pushing back with "this is too much for Mid-I" will find no answer in the document.

### Findings
- **[high]** No priority order or cut line inside Iteration 1 (§8.1; addendum §A status column). All 31 FRs are equally in scope, yet most market FRs are unstarted or draft. Without a ranking, story creation will treat FR-21 (Symbol directory), FR-28 (corporate actions) and FR-30 (replay creation) as just as mandatory as FR-23/25/26, which carry the demo. *Fix:* split §8.1 into Must (the SM-1/SM-2 spine: FR-1–4, FR-13–16, FR-20, FR-22–26, FR-29), Should and Stretch. Name the first three items to cut if the market service is not on TEST by a stated date.
- **[high]** SM-4 cannot be met or verified as written (§9 SM-4; FR-10 assumption; FR-11; OQ1). It needs 10 trading days of scheduled 22:30 and 06:00 UTC runs "on the CI host" before Mid-I. But the shared server is "not provisioned" (addendum §A, FR-10). Turn-taking laptops would have to be online at 03:30 and 11:00 PKT every night. FR-22 is "Not started", and the Mid-I date is unknown. *Fix:* make SM-4 conditional on the Oracle host being up, or measure it over N consecutive runs on whatever host is live. State the latest date the scheduler must be running for SM-4 to be achievable, and add it as a dependency in OQ1/OQ6.
- **[medium]** SM-3 conflicts with the frontend-only fallback (§9 SM-3 vs §8.2 "Frontend-only ACC and PROD are acceptable for `v0.1.0`"; §4.1 Notes). SM-3 requires "PROD and ACC serve the same Content hashes TEST verified". TEST runs `platform` and `market`, and a frontend-only PROD cannot serve those hashes. *Fix:* write SM-3 per Component ("for every Component deployed to PROD…"), or add an explicit frontend-only variant of the target.

## Substance over theater — strong
The Vision (§1) is specific to this product. "A late vendor correction becomes a visible revision rather than a silent overwrite" and the Mid-I demo paragraph could not be swapped into another PRD. The NFRs are not boilerplate: §5 maps each SPEC NFR to an FR or a measured target. The feature NFRs are concrete ("the Jenkins controller has no Docker access"). The personas mirror the SPEC's four roles plus delivery roles, and each one drives a journey or a role check (the viewer drives FR-16 and the UJ-1 refusal). No innovation theater: FR-21 is honestly labelled "New in this PRD".

### Findings
- **[low]** "CI host" is listed as a user (§2.1 Delivery users). It is a machine/role, and its "job" duplicates FR-11/FR-12 rather than adding user insight. *Fix:* fold it into Developer ("whoever is hosting this week") or move it to the Glossary only.
- **[low]** NFR-PERF-003 has a threshold but no pass/fail (§5: "Measured once on `instrument` inserts; result recorded"). If audit triggers add 40%, nothing happens. *Fix:* state what a breach triggers (an Open Question or a known deviation in the Mid report), or drop the ≤20% figure.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is explicit in §1: every later module is "only as trustworthy as that data and as the process that ships it", so Iteration 1 builds those two foundations. §4.2 justifies the platform foundation as a dependency of ingestion (MID §3.4). The success metrics validate the thesis rather than activity. SM-2 (exact planted-fault match) and SM-1 (re-run adds nothing) test trustworthiness directly. The counter-metrics SM-C1–C4 are well chosen; SM-C1 ("A rule that rejects real defects is working") is a good example. The MVP kind (platform foundation) matches the scope logic.

The arc weakens at the edges of §4.3. FR-21, FR-28 and FR-30 are included for reasons other than the thesis: FR-21 is new, FR-28 is included because it is "at no extra cost", and FR-30 seems to exist so SM-1 can show "inverted Replay session refused". Given how much of the market engine is unstarted, these read like backlog items attached to the core.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Scope additions beyond the SPEC are not weighed against capacity (§4.3 FR-21 "New in this PRD"; FR-28 "Extended, included because the Vendor supplies it at no extra cost"; FR-30). Free data is not free engineering: FR-28 drives adjusted series, FR-27's adjusted returns and the outlier split-day exemption. FR-30 adds a DB constraint and a contract schema for a feature with no playback until Iteration 2. *Fix:* for each one, state the thesis-level reason or move it to Should/Stretch under the cut line from Decision-readiness. Drop the inverted-replay step from SM-1 if FR-30 moves.

## Done-ness clarity — strong
This is the PRD's best dimension. Nearly every FR has bounded, checkable consequences: "HTTP 202 with the run id within 800 ms", "up to 4 attempts in total. A `Retry-After` header is honoured", "received = passed untouched + corrected + duplicates removed + rejected (property test)", "match a hand-computed fixture to 10 significant digits", "An UPDATE, DELETE or TRUNCATE on the audit table fails, even when run by the table owner". The Cleaning gate table (FR-25) is directly implementable. There are no "gracefully" or "user-friendly" phrases. The gaps are narrow.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Dividend handling has no testable consequence or method (FR-27 "split- and dividend-adjusted prices"; FR-28 consequences cover only splits). There is no statement of how dividends are recorded, what adjustment convention is used (back-adjusted ratio? vendor-supplied adjusted close?), or what a dividend day must look like in the adjusted return. UJ-2 promises Bilal "adjusted for splits and dividends". *Fix:* add an FR-28 consequence: "A cash dividend creates one Corporate action with amount > 0; adjusted prices before the ex-date are scaled by (1 − D/close_prev)", or "use the Vendor's adjusted fields", plus a fixture check in FR-27.
- **[medium]** Trading calendar source is unspecified, though three behaviours depend on it (Glossary "Trading calendar"; FR-25 `non_trading_day`, session close timestamps "which handles daylight-saving changes and early closes"; FR-25 Data gap rule; FR-22 window "5 Sessions"). The addendum names every other technical choice but not this one, and the holiday/early-close data must come from somewhere free. *Fix:* name the calendar source in addendum §C (for example the `exchange_calendars` library, pinned). Add a consequence that a known early close (day after Thanksgiving) gets the correct UTC close timestamp.
- **[low]** Sign-in lockout scope is undefined (FR-15 "After 5 failed attempts, sign-in is refused for 15 minutes"). Per account, per IP, or both? Per-account lockout lets anyone lock the only admin out during the demo. *Fix:* state the key (for example per username + client IP).
- **[low]** FR-12 assumes a host that may not exist ("The shared CI host backs up Jenkins state nightly"). It is unclear whether turn-taking mode (FR-11), the current operating mode, must also back up. *Fix:* say whether the FR-11 R2 hand-over snapshot satisfies FR-12 while no shared host exists.
- **[low]** "Standard reads" is not defined (FR-29, §5 NFR-PERF-002). The FR-29 bound names one case (1-year window, one Instrument). It is unclear whether run detail and data-quality summary are covered. *Fix:* list the endpoints the 800 ms p95 applies to.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Omissions are explicit and well placed. §7 Non-Goals does real work: no paid feed, no second vendor, no frontend beyond the demo. `[NON-GOAL for Iteration 1]` sits in §4.3 Notes, §8.2 lists the deferrals with target iterations, and the frontend-only ACC/PROD fallback is de-scoped openly. The Assumptions Index round-trips exactly (10 inline, 10 indexed). The open-items count (9 OQs + 10 assumptions + 2 PM notes) is reasonable for a Mid-review planning document. Two gaps undercut this.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The deferred Core NFRs point to a section that does not hold them (§5: "each one is listed in §9 with its target iteration"). §9 is Success Metrics. The NFRs appear only in §10 OQ8, without target iterations. NFR-SEC-002 and NFR-AUD-003 are missing from §8.2, and NFR-AUD-003 has no target at all. For an evaluator checking SPEC Core coverage, this is the one place the PRD's honesty is hard to follow. *Fix:* add all three to §8.2 with a proposed target iteration and correct the cross-reference.
- **[medium]** The "everything free" constraint has untagged card or licence exposures (§6 Cost; FR-11 "free cloud storage"; addendum §C "Cloudflare R2 hand-over"). The Oracle card is tagged as an assumption, but R2 normally requires a payment method on file to enable, even within its free tier, and this is not tagged. The Tiingo free tier is also personal-use. §2.2 mentions it, but the PRD does not confirm that a university team project qualifies. *Fix:* add `[ASSUMPTION]` tags for the R2 payment method (or name a no-card alternative) and for Tiingo terms covering academic team use. Index both.

## Downstream usability — strong
The Glossary (§3) is thorough and mostly applied consistently, with capitalized terms (Bar, Run item, Cleaning gate, Content hash). FR-1–31, UJ-1–7, SM-1–8 and SM-C1–C4 are contiguous and unique. FRs cite UJs, SMs cite FRs, and addendum §A maps every FR to SPEC/MID IDs and tasks, which is what BMAD story creation and the MID §4.7 traceability table need. Each UJ has a named protagonist. UJ-7's protagonist is the scheduler, which is acceptable for a system journey, and it resolves to Zara. Sections can be read on their own.

### Findings
- **[low]** "release" is used against its Glossary meaning (Glossary: "Release — A PROD deploy tagged `v<VERSION>`"; FR-5 "accept or reject the release" on ACC; FR-6 "previously recorded release" for ACC/PROD; Glossary "Rollback"). Story extraction will conflate the two. *Fix:* add a term such as "Deployment record" for an Environment's last good deploy, and keep "Release" for tagged PROD deploys.
- **[low]** "Approver — A User of Jenkins" collides with "User — A person with a QuantSim account" (§3). *Fix:* "a team member with the Jenkins Approver role".

## Shape fit — strong
The PRD is a hybrid: an internal capability spec (pipeline, ingestion) with a small set of multi-role users. It is shaped to match. UJs are used where roles differ (admin, quant, analyst, developer, approver) and add the demo and outage paths, which are what evaluators will watch. FRs carry the precision. It is chain-top (feeding epics/stories and OpenSpec), so the heavy traceability is justified. Moving technical choices into the addendum keeps the PRD at capability level, with a few leaks noted below. Not over-formalized for a university FYP; if anything, its rigor exceeds what a Mid review requires, which is fine.

## Mechanical notes
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** clean. 10 inline tags (FR-1, FR-4, FR-10, §4.1 Free NFR, §4.1 Notes, FR-17, FR-20, FR-21, FR-22, §6) match 10 index entries.
- **ID continuity:** FR-1–31, UJ-1–7, SM-1–8 and SM-C1–4 have no gaps or duplicates. Addendum decisions are renamed I-D6–I-D8 to avoid a collision, which is good.
- **Broken cross-reference:** §5 "listed in §9" should point to §8.2/§10 (see Scope honesty).
- **Target mismatch to reconcile:** FR-1 assumes a 15-minute check bound, while SM-5 targets a ≤10-minute median. They are compatible (bound vs median), but say so to avoid a story using the wrong number.
- **Addendum status inconsistency:** addendum §A marks FR-6 rollback "Verified with a deliberately broken release", while §4.1 says ACC "failed its first run" and PROD was "never run". State where rollback was verified.
- **Technical leakage despite the §0 convention:** FR-31 names "Redis", and FR-4 names `pr.sh promote` / the git-flow skill. Both are minor; they can stay if they are intentional evidence pointers.
- **SM-4 phrasing:** "succeeded on at least 9 days … the other day ends `partially_succeeded`" reads as requiring exactly one partial day. Reword to "and at most one day ends `partially_succeeded`".
- **Required sections:** all present for the stakes (Vision, users, UJs, Glossary, FRs, NFRs, constraints, non-goals, scope, SMs with counter-metrics, OQs, assumptions).
